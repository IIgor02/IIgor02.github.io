About

Research profile and fields.

I am an M.Sc. student in Economics at CAEN/UFC. My research interests are in Econometrics, Poverty and Inequality, and Development Economics, with a focus on quantile regressions, spatial econometrics, policy evaluation, and regional development.

I am also a researcher at the Ceará Development Agency (ADECE), where I work primarily on socioeconomic indicators for public-policy analysis and on the evaluation of public policies.

My work combines methodological and applied research, with an emphasis on empirical questions relevant to developing economies. I am particularly interested in using distributional, spatial, and panel-data methods to study regional disparities and evaluate public policies.

I hold a B.A. in Economics from the Federal University of Ceará, where I graduated summa cum laude. My research draws on administrative data and survey microdata to address policy-relevant economic questions.

Common-Shock-Robust Inference for Extreme Panel Quantiles

In Revision Econometrics and Statistics Econometrics Extreme Quantiles Common Shocks

Proposes an inference framework for extrapolated extreme panel quantiles that accounts for synchronized common shocks with heterogeneous exposure across units, with an empirical illustration using food-inflation risk.

Read current abstract

Extreme conditional quantiles in panel data are commonly obtained by extrapolating from intermediate quantile estimates, an approach that becomes fragile when many units are pushed into the tail by the same aggregate events. In such settings, pervasive common shocks do not only create cross-sectional dependence in the final statistic; they may also enter the intermediate anchor, distort the local spacing used for extrapolation, and concentrate the residual exceedances from which tail shape is learned. This paper develops an inference procedure for extrapolated extreme panel quantiles that is robust to synchronized shocks with heterogeneous exposure across units. The method defines a common-correlated-effects proxy target, estimates CCE-augmented fixed-effects quantile anchors, and derives a joint expansion that carries anchor, spacing, and tail-index uncertainty into the final extreme quantile through a time-cluster multiplier. The Monte Carlo evidence shows that, when common-shock exposure is heterogeneous, interacted CCE adjustments substantially improve coverage, reduce prediction error, and prevent residual tail events from being dominated by a small number of dates. An empirical illustration using monthly food-inflation acceleration in 51 developing economies shows that accounting for common shocks lowers measured aggregate q99 tail risk relative to unadjusted extrapolation, while preserving a clear development-vulnerability gradient across countries.
