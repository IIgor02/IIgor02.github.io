Research profile.

I am an M.Sc. student in Economics at CAEN/UFC and currently applying to PhD programs in Economics. My research interests are in Econometrics, Poverty and Inequality, and Development Economics, with a focus on quantile regressions, spatial econometrics, policy evaluation, and regional development.

Regime-Dependent Spatial Multipliers in Panel Data: A Threshold Spatial Durbin Approach

with Fabrício Carneiro Linhares

M.Sc. thesis.

Develops a threshold spatial Durbin panel framework in which spatial dependence and covariate effects can vary across regimes, with estimation, bias correction, and inference for regime-dependent spatial multipliers.

Keywords: Spatial econometrics · Panel data · Threshold models

Abstract

This paper develops quasi-maximum likelihood (QML) estimation and inference for a threshold Spatial Durbin panel model with unit fixed effects, in which the spatial-lag coefficient, the covariate slopes, and their spatial-lag coefficients all switch jointly at an unknown threshold. The threshold also modifies the spatial multiplier through which shocks propagate, so the model permits regime-contingent direct, indirect, and total effects. Estimation proceeds by profile QML after removing the unit effects via the within transformation, with analytical corrections for the finite-sample bias in the regular parameter estimates and for the degrees-of-freedom distortion in the estimated disturbance variance. Under a large N, large T framework, we establish consistency and asymptotic normality for the regular parameters, derive the nonstandard convergence rate of the threshold estimator, construct likelihood-ratio confidence sets for it, and propose a bootstrap supremum test for parameter homogeneity. Monte Carlo experiments show that the bias corrections materially improve variance estimation and inferential accuracy, and that the corrected QML estimator performs favorably relative to a homogeneous Spatial Durbin model, a non-spatial panel threshold model, and a correctly specified spatial two-stage least squares estimator, with the largest gains for the regime-specific spatial coefficients and the threshold location. An application to sixty economies observed over four decades identifies an initial-income threshold near eight thousand five hundred dollars per capita, above which estimated growth transmission through the spatial network is substantially stronger than below it, a form of heterogeneity that neither a homogeneous spatial model nor a non-spatial threshold specification can represent.

Common-Shock-Robust Inference for Extreme Panel Quantiles

In revision at Econometrics and Statistics.

Proposes an inference framework for extrapolated extreme panel quantiles that accounts for synchronized common shocks with heterogeneous exposure across units, with an empirical illustration using food-inflation risk.

Keywords: Econometrics · Extreme quantiles · Common shocks

Abstract

Extreme conditional quantiles in panel data are commonly obtained by extrapolating from intermediate quantile estimates, an approach that becomes fragile when many units are pushed into the tail by the same aggregate events. In such settings, pervasive common shocks do not only create cross-sectional dependence in the final statistic; they may also enter the intermediate anchor, distort the local spacing used for extrapolation, and concentrate the residual exceedances from which tail shape is learned. This paper develops an inference procedure for extrapolated extreme panel quantiles that is robust to synchronized shocks with heterogeneous exposure across units. The method defines a common-correlated-effects proxy target, estimates CCE-augmented fixed-effects quantile anchors, and derives a joint expansion that carries anchor, spacing, and tail-index uncertainty into the final extreme quantile through a time-cluster multiplier. The Monte Carlo evidence shows that, when common-shock exposure is heterogeneous, interacted CCE adjustments substantially improve coverage, reduce prediction error, and prevent residual tail events from being dominated by a small number of dates. An empirical illustration using monthly food-inflation acceleration in 51 developing economies shows that accounting for common shocks lowers measured aggregate q99 tail risk relative to unadjusted extrapolation, while preserving a clear development-vulnerability gradient across countries.

Who Bears the Burden of Monetary Tightening? Credit Default Responses Across Income Groups and Firm Sizes in Brazil

with Ramon Lima Ribeiro and Victor L. Stilita

Published in International Economics and Economic Policy (2026).

DOI
Abstract

This paper examines heterogeneous monetary transmission to credit default in Brazil, exploiting granular disaggregation by income bracket, firm size, and state. Using monthly Central Bank data from 2012 to 2024 covering all 27 Brazilian states, we document heterogeneous predictive responses across households and firms. In the baseline Selic-path estimates, household responses are strongest in the middle of the income distribution, particularly across the 1–5 minimum-wage brackets, while responses at higher incomes are smaller; this household ordering is not recovered with comparable precision in the exercise based on unexpected Selic target changes. On the corporate side, small firms display the clearest baseline responses, emerging after roughly 10–14 months, while micro firms also react but with less precise aggregate baseline evidence. The surprise-based exercise provides clearer support on the firm side, and additional sectoral and product-level results point to institutional credit segmentation, with free and short-term corporate credit responding more strongly than more protected categories. Overall, the evidence is strongest for firm-side heterogeneity, while the household pattern is best interpreted as a suggestive reduced-form result of the baseline analysis.

Accepted for Publication

Enhancing State GDP Projections with MIDAS and Google Trends: Evidence from Ceará

with Fabrício C. Linhares

Accepted for publication in Revista Econômica do Nordeste (2025).

Abstract

This study investigates the use of variables with mixed temporal frequencies – monthly and quarterly, including Google Trends indices, to forecast the quarterly and annual Gross Domestic Product (GDP) of the state of Ceará. Recent studies show that incorporating high-frequency variables and alternative indicators, such as Google Trends data, improves forecasts by allowing real-time updates and capturing consumption and behavior trends before traditional economic indicators. To assess this approach, the study employs four variants of the Mixed Data Sampling (MIDAS) model. The results indicate that the inclusion of Google Trends indices improves predictive performance. Additionally, the forecasts from the MIDAS and Auto-Regressive MIDAS (AR-MIDAS) models with Google Trends are found to perform similarly or better than the official projections of the Institute for Research and Economic Strategy of Ceará (IPECE). These findings highlight the importance of state investment in expanding its capacity to forecast key economic indicators. More accurate projections enable the government to adjust revenues and expenditures more efficiently, prevent fiscal deficits, and support investment planning and the formulation of policies aimed at economic development.

Sectoral Productive Shocks and Poverty Reduction in Brazilian Microregions

Accepted for publication in Revista Brasileira de Estudos Regionais e Urbanos (2026).

Abstract

This paper estimates the reduced-form effect of Brazilian microregions’ exposure to national sectoral shocks on poverty from 2000 to 2010. Using a shift-share design based on predetermined sectoral composition, a one-standard-deviation increase in predicted wage-bill exposure reduces the poverty headcount by 0.48 percentage points, the poverty gap by 0.46 pp, and poverty severity by 0.40 pp. The estimates are stable across official microregions, alternative Bartik weights, state-clustered and shift-share-robust standard errors, and the exclusion of refined petroleum. Permutation inference is more conservative, and Rotemberg weights show that identification is concentrated in extractive and petroleum-related sectors. We therefore interpret the results as reduced-form evidence on the local poverty incidence of sectoral exposure, not as proof that broad formalization is the causal channel.

Under Review

Nowcasting Brazilian Quarterly GDP: Mixed-Frequency Econometric and Machine Learning Models Under Multiple Performance Criteria

Under review at Journal of Forecasting.

Abstract

This paper evaluates a real-time system for estimating current Brazilian quarterly GDP that combines monthly economic data with different forecasting methods. Using a simulated real-time environment over 2015Q1–2025Q1 and four forecast horizons (current quarter through three quarters ahead), we compare ten forecasting approaches under three data scenarios: a comprehensive panel of monthly indicators and two smaller panels selected using automated variable selection techniques (LASSO and OCMT). We evaluate performance using criteria relevant for policy institutions: forecast precision, ability to predict economic direction and recessions, detection of downside risks and low-growth periods, and statistical comparisons across models. The results show that model performance rankings change depending on which data are available and when during the quarter the forecast is made. With the full dataset, a mixed-frequency LASSO model (sgLASSO–MIDAS) delivers the best average accuracy, while LASSO variable selection favors simpler linear models like Elastic Net. Under OCMT selection, mixed-frequency econometric models remain competitive and threshold models perform particularly well during economic crises. Statistical tests indicate that several model configurations perform similarly, supporting a diversified approach guided by practical implementation and institutional monitoring objectives. Overall, effective real-time GDP monitoring in Brazil benefits from aligning forecasting methods, data selection, and the specific needs faced by policy institutions.

Water Storage Capacity and Child Health under Drought in Brazil

with Ramon Lima Ribeiro and João Mário Santos de França

Accepted for presentation at the XXIV National Meeting of the Brazilian Association of Regional and Urban Studies (ENABER), 2026.

Abstract

This paper examines whether municipal water storage capacity reduces the vulnerability of child health to drought in Brazil. Its main contribution is to measure water infrastructure through the physical storage capacity available per vulnerable population and compare this measure with installed cistern counts. To examine this relationship, we combine monthly data from 2008 to 2022 on hospitalizations of children under five for infectious intestinal diseases, satellite rainfall, formal water service coverage, and administrative records of social water technologies. We then estimate a spatial panel quantile model that captures differences across municipalities, spatial dependence, and unobserved common shocks. Among municipalities with long-run mean precipitation below 1,000 millimeters, each additional 1,000 cubic meters of storage capacity per 1,000 children is associated with a reduction of 0.724 to 0.751 hospitalizations per 10,000 children in the median response to drought. Moreover, after accounting for formal water coverage and its interaction with drought, the corresponding attenuation ranges from 3.120 to 3.422 at the 0.90 quantile, which indicates that storage capacity becomes particularly relevant where morbidity is higher. Estimates based on cistern counts are also negative, but they remain imprecise. Taken together, the results show that storage capacity per vulnerable population provides a more informative measure of local adaptive capacity, with relevance for protecting child health and, through that channel, human development in drought-exposed regions.

Public Development Credit and Agricultural Resilience to Drought: Evidence from Brazil’s Semi-Arid Northeast

Ramon Lima Ribeiro, Christiano Modesto Penna, Pedro Igor Oliveira, and Felipe de Sousa Bastos

Accepted for presentation at the ANPEC National Meeting, 2026.

Abstract

Public credit may be classified as climate-related, but in semi-arid regions its development value depends on whether it protects production when drought occurs. This paper investigates whether municipal entry into the direct climate-credit lines of Brazil’s Northeast Constitutional Financing Fund (FNE) reduces agricultural losses caused by rainfall shocks. We apply a staggered-entry difference-in-differences design to the drought-growth relationship, estimating how drought sensitivity changes after credit entry. Two or more years after entry, a one-standard-deviation drought innovation generates about 2.6 log points less agricultural-growth loss. Complementary results show stronger growth in crops that dominated municipal production before treatment, while annual land-conversion flows remain close to zero. For development banks, the findings underscore the importance of combining territorial targeting, local capacity and evaluation of productive performance under drought.

Intersectional Lower-Tail Wage Rigidity in Brazil: Evidence from Census Data and Synthetic Cohorts

with Ramon Lima Ribeiro

Accepted for presentation at the ANPEC Regional Meeting, 2026.

Abstract

This paper documents intersectional lower-tail wage rigidity in Brazil, defined strictly as a distributional pattern across groups rather than as institutional wage stickiness or individual dynamic entrapment. Using the 2010 Brazilian Demographic Census, we compare workers across race/color, gender, and Northeast residence and ask whether disadvantage appears not only in average wages, but also in bottom-tail representation, quantile-specific penalties, nonlinear wage gradients, and wage-structure components. The empirical strategy combines descriptive bottom-decile measures, conditional quantile regressions, RIF-based unconditional quantile regressions, threshold-style quantile models, and RIF decompositions, complemented by PNAD Contínua synthetic cohorts for 2012–2023. Northeast Black/Brown women and men are disproportionately represented in the bottom decile, face large lower-tail penalties, and display persistent cohort-level Q10 gaps across repeated cross-sections. The results provide a coherent set of distributional and cohort-level facts consistent with mechanisms emphasized in the persistent-poverty, inequality-of-opportunity, and regional-development literatures.

Water Infrastructure and Drought Resilience in the Brazilian Semi-Arid: Evidence from the São Francisco River Integration Project

Ramon Lima Ribeiro and Pedro Igor Araujo de Oliveira

Accepted for presentation at the ANPEC Regional Meeting, 2026.

Abstract

This paper evaluates whether the São Francisco River Integration Project (PISF) reduced the economic penalty of drought in the Brazilian semi-arid region. Its central contribution is to study the transfer project not only as a regional investment with average effects, but as an adaptation policy capable of changing the drought-damage function. The empirical strategy exploits the project’s operational rollout: within the official universe of beneficiary municipalities, some had functional access to PISF water at the beginning of the operational period, while others depended on future branches, conveyance pipelines, reservoirs, state distribution systems or later integration stages. Identification combines drought intensity, PISF exposure, the post-2017 period and historical drought vulnerability, using a municipal panel with CHIRPS rainfall, PAM/IBGE agricultural production, RAIS formal employment and the institutional and spatial geography of the project. In the main damage-function specification, which absorbs municipality-specific drought sensitivity, early PISF exposure significantly attenuates the drought penalty on real agricultural production value in historically vulnerable municipalities. A 100-km spatial exposure measure corroborates the primary result and suggests lower crop concentration, although effects on formal employment and physical productivity are less stable. The evidence indicates that the economic value of the transfer project is more clearly detected as reduced losses during dry years than as a homogeneous growth shock.

Text-Based Financial Stress and Sovereign Risk in Brazil

with Victor L. Stilita and Ramon Lima Ribeiro

Accepted for presentation at the Brazilian Finance Congress, 2026.

Abstract

This paper develops three text-based indicators of financial stress for Brazil using Google Trends search data and financial news from major Brazilian outlets between 2008 and 2025. The first indicator is based on a curated dictionary of Portuguese-language search terms, the second uses a machine-learning selection procedure, and the third is built from 33,881 news articles. We assess their empirical relevance for Brazil’s 5-year sovereign CDS spread using local projections, Granger-causality tests, and regime-switching analysis. The dictionary-based index shows the most stable association with subsequent CDS widening. The news-based measure also contains useful information, especially in the Granger exercises, while the machine-learning specification is less consistent in this application. The results suggest that search-based and news-based indicators can complement conventional tools for monitoring sovereign financial stress in Brazil.

Work in Progress

Distributional Functionals in Fixed-Effects Panels: A Weighted-Average Quantile Approach

Abstract

This article develops weighted-average quantile regression (WAQR) for fixed-effects panels when the object of interest is not a mean or an isolated quantile, but an integrated feature of the conditional distribution. The method is designed for questions about expected shortfall, lower-tail welfare, inequality-sensitive welfare, and distributional spread, where conventional fixed-effects regressions and pointwise panel quantile regressions answer different, and often incomplete, questions. WAQR estimates a smoothed fixed-effects quantile process and maps it into an orthogonal transformed outcome whose within-regression coefficient targets the chosen distributional functional. Cross-fitting and leave-one-out nuisance evaluation eliminate first-order own-observation feedback, while split-panel jackknife corrections remove the remaining incidental-parameter bias. For the normalized smoothed fixed-effects quantile first step, the leading two-way bias has additive 1/T and 1/n components and is removed by a full-panel, time-split, and cross-section-split combination. The resulting estimator is √nT-asymptotically normal and supports cluster-robust inference without estimating conditional densities. Simulations verify the bias correction and show that naive integration of fixed-effects quantile slopes can target the wrong estimand. Applications to international welfare and U.S. industry-portfolio returns show economically meaningful differences between mean, lower-tail, expected-shortfall, and spread effects.
